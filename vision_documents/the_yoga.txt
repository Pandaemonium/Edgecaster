Edgecaster Yoga Treatise
Observer-Driven Reality, Absolute Space, and Scale-Aware Simulation

This document is meant to be copied into future threads as the “north star” for Edgecaster’s architecture. It captures the philosophy (“the yoga”), why we’re doing it, and the practical rules that keep us from slipping back into spaghetti “zone metaphysics.”

1) The Core Problem We Are Solving

Edgecaster began with a traditional roguelike “zone/chunk” worldview:

The world is partitioned into rectangular zones.

Entities and terrain are stored and simulated primarily in zone-local coordinates.

Transitioning zones triggers spawn, cleanup, and other behavior.

Rendering and targeting depend on “what zone you’re in.”

This is a dead-end for Edgecaster’s actual ambition:

Multi-scale world (planetary/cosmic → regional → local → microscopic)

Fractal/continuous map logic (fields sampled continuously)

“Quantum observer” gameplay (what you observe becomes real)

Aggregate entities that resolve and coarsen dynamically

The same underlying bug kept surfacing in many disguises:

cursors stuck to player / targeting desync

overlay effects invisible

UI transitions flying in from (0,0)

patterns “forgetting” across boundaries

entities vanishing despite being in view

These weren’t separate bugs. They were authority mismatches:
different systems disagreed about what coordinate space was “real.”

2) Canonical Truths (Non-Negotiable)
2.1 Absolute space is truth

Every entity, effect, tile sample, and interaction must ultimately be representable in absolute world coordinates:

abs_x, abs_y (and a notion of scale)

Anything else is a cache or derived view

Zones may exist, but only as:

memory/performance caches

data containers

streaming boundaries

They are not metaphysical.

Rule of thumb

If something changes behavior at a zone boundary, that’s a bug unless explicitly designed as a streaming limitation.

2.2 Rendering projects reality — it does not invent it

The renderer’s job is:

take canonical world data (ABS + scale)

project to screen through camera transform

apply FoV/visibility rules

It should never be forced to “guess” or “repair” broken game state.

If something is being drawn wrong:

it’s usually not “rendering is wrong”

it’s the renderer exposing a worldview mismatch upstream

2.3 Observation defines perception

Edgecaster is an observer-driven game:

The camera + attention field defines what must be realized in high fidelity.

The rest of the world can be abstracted, coarsened, or represented as aggregates.

But: observation affects representation, not retroactive truth.

If you possess a bird and fly 1000 km away:

your human vessel doesn’t cease to exist

it becomes cold/abstracted

when you return, it’s still there (modulo time passing or events)

2.4 Observation Selects Simulation Regime (Not Just Representation)

A common misunderstanding is that “the camera must not create gameplay state.”

This is only half true.

The correct rule is:

The camera must not invent arbitrary or irreversible world state —
but it does select the simulation regime under which the world advances when time ticks.

Zooming or panning alone must not:

create entities

destroy entities

apply deltas

However, when time advances, the world advances at the fidelity implied by the current observation scale.

Consequence:

If the player zooms into a battle and presses “advance time,”
soldiers and arrows visible at that scale are real and simulated.

Zooming back out coarsens the simulation, but must preserve outcomes deterministically.

This is observer-driven reality, not camera-driven hallucination.

Camera motion may stage/unstage entities without advancing time.
Zoom/pan/look should be allowed to instantiate and de-instantiate entities purely to set the stage (e.g., berry_patch ⇄ berries), even while paused. This does not violate “camera must not invent irreversible world state,” because staging does not apply deltas or advance simulation; it only ensures that if time advances now, the correct ontology is present.

Practical rule:

Render must never realize entities.

Lifecycle may stage/unstage entities based on attention.

Time advancement commits changes only through simulation and player actions.

3) “The Player Is Not Special” (and the subtlety)

The player entity should be “just another entity”:

same data model

same spawn pipeline

same coordinate rules

same rendering rules

What is special is the observer:

the camera/attention field

the currently inhabited actor id

what is simulated at what fidelity

This matters because eventually:

you can body-swap

the “player” moves from human → bird → demon → city-avatar

the observer follows, not the original vessel

So:

entities are not special

observation is special

4) Scale is Real: Entities Exist on a Continuum

Edgecaster isn’t one grid. It’s a multi-scale cosmos.

A cricket, a player, a city, and a Leviathan can all exist in the same “world,” but at different scales.

Canonical position is continuous

An entity’s “abs position” is meaningful only alongside scale:

the “tile” you use depends on the LoD / scale context

coarse entities (cities) occupy many fine tiles

fine entities (crickets) exist within the footprint of coarse tiles

Implication

Caching/performance buckets must be scale-dependent.

Old rigid rectangles (“zones”) are:

arbitrary

scale-blind

incompatible with a Leviathan spanning many zones

meaningless for tiny entities where a zone is enormous

So the long-term replacement is:

A dynamic, nested “attention field grid” (multi-level) that tracks what should be realized at each scale near the observer.

5) The Attention Field: The Future Replacement for Zones

Zones were an attempt at:

memory containment

generation boundaries

But Edgecaster needs a continuous model:

The attention field is a spatial/scale heatmap

hottest: near camera AND similar scale

colder: far away OR wrong scale

Examples:

zoomed in: berries and crickets matter

zoomed out: only berry groves / forests matter

scrying a far battle: battalions tick, not individual soldiers

far away: war resolves as abstract dice-roll outcomes

This implies a hierarchy of entity representations

city → districts → buildings → walls → NPCs

forest → woods → trees → berry patches → berries

army → battalions → squads → individuals

And a system of deltas / persistent state:

if you pick a berry, that delta must persist

regeneration can happen later when cold

if you kill a bear, it stays dead (unless designed otherwise)

if a bear wanders 3 screens away, that displacement persists

Section 5.5 — Macro Entities Are Not Just “Big Entities”

(Insert after Section 5: The Attention Field)

Macro entities are ontologically different from local entities.

A city, forest, site, or army is not simply:

a very large creature, or

a normal entity rendered at a larger scale.

Instead, macro entities are:

summaries of many possible finer states

representations chosen by the observer’s scale

anchors for refinement and coarsening

Key rule:

A macro entity should not be created, destroyed, or multiplied simply because the camera zooms or pans.

If macro entities appear/disappear erratically with camera movement, that indicates:

generation is tied to view mechanics instead of world structure, or

indexing depends on transient buckets rather than canonical identity.

Macro entities must have:

a stable identity (even if cold / abstract)

a stable footprint in absolute space

a defined scale band where they are the preferred representation

Section 5.6 — Camera Attention ≠ World Generation

(Directly addresses what went wrong)

The camera determines what is observed, not what exists.

Therefore:

The camera / attention field may select representations

It must not be the primary driver of world generation

Anti-pattern:

“When the camera enters a region or zoom level, generate entities for that scale.”

This leads to:

density explosions

rings / blooms around the observer

entities that exist only because they were looked at

Correct model:

World structure exists independently (even if abstracted)

The camera selects which layer of that structure is rendered or simulated

Observation chooses fidelity, not existence.

Important clarification

“Camera attention ≠ world generation” does not mean that the camera is inert.

It means:

the camera must not be the source of entities

but it is allowed to select which simulation model is authoritative when time advances

The camera chooses how the world is simulated, not whether it exists.

Section 5.7 — Stable Buckets Before Fractal Buckets

(This is the big lesson from the failed attempt)

Before introducing fully fractal, multi-LoD spatial buckets, the system must satisfy this invariant:

An entity’s membership in a spatial container must not change solely due to zoom level.

In practice, this means:

Do not re-index entities into different spatial buckets every frame

Do not rebuild global indices as camera scale changes

Do not let “cell size” drift with zoom unless the entity itself is changing representation

Transitional guideline:

Prefer fixed, absolute-space buckets (even if coarse)

Layer scale logic on top of a stable spatial index

Only introduce nested / fractal buckets once:

representation ladders are explicit

refinement/coarsening is deterministic

identity is preserved across transitions

This avoids “thrashing” where entities vanish, duplicate, or reset.

Section 5.8 — Representation Ladders Are Explicit, Not Emergent

(Clarifies elephants vs humans vs cities, etc.)

Entities that can exist at multiple scales must declare a representation ladder.

Example:

forest
→ woods
→ tree clusters
→ trees
→ berries

This ladder is:

explicit (declared in data or code)

ordered

reversible (coarsening is as real as refinement)

Rule:

An entity should never “fade into nothing” when crossing a scale boundary.
It must either:

remain as a token, or

hand off to a parent/child representation.

If zooming causes an entity to disappear without replacement, the ladder is incomplete.


Observed = Real Is a Valid Mode (Proxies Are Optional)

Edgecaster supports (and currently prefers) the following rule:

If an entity is visible at a given scale and time advances, it is real at that scale.

Under this doctrine:

Zooming into a battle before pressing “advance time” performs deterministic realization.

Pressing “advance time” causes arrows to fly, soldiers to move, and spells to interact at that fidelity.

Zooming out coarsens the simulation but must preserve results deterministically.

Render-only proxies are not mandatory.

They are an optional optimization for:

explanatory detail without commitment

performance budgets at extreme densities

Future contributors must not assume proxies are inevitable.
The baseline model is:

Observed entities are real when time advances.

Proxies are optional — and turning them off is a useful truth test.
Render-only detail proxies can make the game “look right” while the ontology is wrong (entities visible but not interactable, not inspectable, not removable, not persistent). During yoga work, it can be valuable to disable proxies entirely so the world becomes honest: if berries/soldiers are visible, they must exist as real entities in LevelState.entities and participate in “You see here…”, look/inspect, pickup/removal, and persistence.

This is the canonical first-pass doctrine for Edgecaster:
if you can see it at the active scale, it is real (and 'real' means it goes through the canonical spawn path, can be queried, and can be modified by deltas). Proxies may return later only as an optimization.

New Section 5.9 — Attention Budget Is a Selection Problem, Not a Creation Problem

The attention system’s job is:

deciding what to simulate

deciding what to render

deciding what to defer

It is not responsible for:

creating entities

destroying entities

redefining world structure

Budgets apply to:

simulation ticks

render proxies

detail resolution

They must never apply to:

canonical identity

persistence

world history

This distinction prevents:

“player disappears because too many berries”

“entities vanish when stepping off a zone”

simulation depending on incidental ordering

Section 5.10 — Warmness Must Govern Candidate Gathering

Key lesson: It is not enough to apply “attention” at the final selection step.
Warmness must be applied before candidate enumeration, or the observer can fall out of reality.

The bug we hit

Even with a smart multi-scale selection step, the player (and other critical entities) could still “disappear” when panning the camera without zooming.

This was not a renderer problem. This was not “too many entities.”

It was a pipeline authority mismatch:

The candidate list was built from a tight camera rect (with float → int boundary effects)

The “warm fovea” logic was applied after candidates existed

So a tiny pan could exclude the player from candidates, and downstream selection could not resurrect them

The invariant

Attention (warmness) must drive candidate gathering.

This means:

Build a warm rect = (camera rect) + padding

Union it with the inhabited actor fovea (player position)

Use this warm rect for:

world-index queries (macro entities)

loaded-zone spatial bin queries (micro entities)

any other pre-filter candidate scan

Once an entity is absent from candidates, it is effectively “nonexistent” for the frame — and this is a yoga violation if the entity is in the observer fovea.

Dirty flags / cache invalidation is yoga-critical.
When lifecycle spawns or despawns entities (or moves them), any spatial bins / fast lookup caches must be invalidated immediately (mark dirty) so that rendering, “You see here…”, look/inspect, and targeting all see the same reality in the same frame. Otherwise you get “half-real” entities: present in level.entities but missing from queries and rendering.

Section 5.11 — Selection Churn Is Not Motion

Symptom

“Entities shift around when panning, without world motion.”

Cause

This can be caused by selection mechanics, not simulation.

If the per-frame selection logic picks “nearest K entities to camera center” inside a cell, then panning changes the ordering, and the visible subset changes each frame. That looks like objects moving, but it’s actually culling churn.

Rule of thumb

Prefer selection orders that are stable under small camera motion.

Example: per-cell selection should rank by distance to cell center, not distance to camera center, unless there’s a compelling reason.

Section 5.12 — Bands Are for Legibility, Not Ontological Guillotines

Symptom

Macro entities (sites/berry patches) vanish entirely when zoomed in, even though they should still be “present” or represented.

Cause

Overly strict band filtering such as:

“entity must be in the one active band” (e_band == b)

This makes adjacent-scale entities disappear without any representation ladder handoff.

Guidance

Until representation ladders are fully implemented, allow a small band overlap (e.g., ±1 band) as a transitional bridge:

Bands remain the main legibility filter

But we avoid hard discontinuities where important entities vanish with no substitute

This is not “overlapping realities”; it is a pragmatic bridge until explicit handoffs exist.

Section 5.13 — World Index Entities Must Render Without Zone Iteration

The scalable rule

When the camera spans many zones, never iterate all zones to gather candidates.

Instead:

Query macro/world entities via a global spatial index in absolute space

Only scan loaded zones (caches) for local entities

Never instantiate zones during rendering

This preserves performance while keeping “world truth” visible at any scale.

6) Transitional Doctrine: Bridges Are Allowed

During migration, it is acceptable to have:

ABS truth

plus local caches/views

As long as the directionality is correct:

Truth → View is always possible
View → Truth must happen whenever the view mutates reality

This prevents:

“two realities” (one per zone)

effects resurrecting after crossing boundaries

UI projection desync

Practical migration pattern

Create canonical state object(s) (ABS)

Make zones/levels view those canonical objects

If legacy code still writes to LevelState, immediately “commit back” to canonical state

Over time, remove legacy writes entirely

7) UI & Targeting Yoga: One Spatial Contract

Input and UI are where the yoga is most fragile.

The contract

mouse and keyboard targeting must agree

cursor should be canonical in ABS tile-space

local cursor is optional “if representable”

You can’t have:

name preview correct but cursor box stuck

arrow keys work but mouse doesn’t

“look” can target but runes can’t

Rule

All targeting state should be stored in ABS (target_cursor_abs).
If you also maintain target_cursor (local), it must be derived.

8) Rune/Overlay Yoga: One Pattern, One Truth

Runecasting is a perfect microcosm of the yoga:

A rune pattern is a world object:

it has geometry

modifiers (subdivide/rainbow/acid/fern/etc.)

motion state (push)

activation state (TTL / preview)

It must exist as a single canonical reality:

one pattern

one anchor

one modifier state

persistent across streaming boundaries

never “different status per zone”

Rule

Rune state must be canonical (ABS).
Zone-level pattern fields are only views/caches.

If a rune disappears, reappears, forgets modifiers, or resurrects after crossing a boundary:

the system is still zone-metaphysical

canonical state is not being updated correctly

9) Practical Debug Heuristics (“How to Smell a Yoga Violation”)

These are the classic symptoms that tell you you’ve violated the worldview:

9.1 “Flies in from upper-left”

UI animation anchored from (0,0) means:

you used zone-local coords

or you used uninitialized coords

or you projected using wrong basis

9.2 Mouse and keyboard disagree

If arrow keys work but mouse doesn’t (or vice versa):

you have two coordinate conversion paths

one of them double-converts or clamps

9.3 Things change at zone boundaries

If something disappears, resets, or resurrects when stepping across boundaries:

canonical state is still zone-owned

or view->truth commit is missing

9.4 Overlays exist logically but not visually

If logs say “Terminus placed” but nothing appears:

the stored data is being overwritten by a “canonical sync”

or the renderer projects it using the wrong coordinate frame

9.5 “Always chooses the rightmost vertex”

Classic sign:

nearest-neighbor selection is running in the wrong coordinate space

often ABS vs local-world mismatch

When debugging “entities disappear / shift / flicker”:

Is the entity present in candidates?
If not: warm rect is wrong or coordinate conversion is wrong.

If yes: is selection stable?
If selection changes with tiny camera motion, you may be ranking by camera center.

If yes: is the entity filtered out by strict band logic?
Consider band overlap as a bridge until ladders exist.

10) The Long Roadmap (High-Level, Not Bug Fixes)

This is the intended sequence of evolution:

Stage 1 — Coordinate authority unification (now)

ABS is truth for entities and world effects

zones become caches/views

targeting uses ABS

UI transitions use real on-screen anchors

Stage 2 — Centralize coordinate transforms

Create helper APIs like:

renderer.screen_to_abs_tile()

renderer.abs_tile_to_screen_px()

game.abs_from_zone_local(...) (temporary bridge)
…and eliminate ad-hoc math scattered across code.

Stage 3 — Attention field caching replaces vestigial zones

dynamic nested grid keyed by scale

“hotness” determines simulation fidelity and representation

deterministic realization/coarsening

persistent deltas

Stage 4 — Macro entity hierarchy

entities can resolve into sub-entities

coarsen back into aggregates

armies, cities, forests become first-class

Stage 5 — Scheduling / simulation budget

a global energy queue whose tick granularity depends on attention field

high fidelity near observer

abstract resolution far away

11) Examples to Keep the Vision Concrete
Example A: Body swap

You possess a bird:

camera follows bird

nearby entities resolve around bird

your old human body remains in the world (cold)

if you return later, it is still there (or changed via events)

Example B: Forest resolution

Far away: one “forest” entity
Closer: splits into “woods” and “clearings”
Closer: trees, berry patches
Closest: individual berries

Picking a berry creates a delta:

patch remembers depletion

regen system may refill later when cold

Example C: Army simulation

Far away: “army” entity (strength, morale)
Near: battalions tick
Very near: soldiers tick

Looking away reduces fidelity but should preserve outcomes deterministically.

Example D: Rune across boundaries

A rune drawn across a former zone seam:

persists

pushes across

modifiers persist

does not reset when you step over an invisible boundary


Example E: Arrows and Wind Wall

The player zooms into a battlefield and sees arrows mid-air.

Zooming alone does not advance time.

The arrows are deterministically realized from battalion state.

If the player casts Wind Wall before advancing time:

the spell applies to those realized arrows

When the player presses “advance time”:

arrows fly

wind deflects them

outcomes persist even if the player zooms back out

The arrows do not “freeze,” teleport, or re-roll.
This is required for a coherent observer-driven world.
