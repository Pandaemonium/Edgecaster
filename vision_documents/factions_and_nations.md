# Edgecaster Factions And Nations Guide

## 1. Scope

This document is for the social and cultural layer of the setting: nations, peoples, and other recurring identity groups with distinct voices or patterns.

It is not a complete census of the continent. It should grow carefully as groups solidify.

## 2. How To Extend This File

When adding a new people, nation, or major faction, try to answer:

- What makes them recognizable?
- What is their aesthetic register?
- How do they talk, trade, travel, fight, worship, or organize?
- What are the common misconceptions about them?
- What variation exists inside the group?

Avoid turning a culture into one joke, one trait, or one costume.

## 3. The Astean Empire

Current confidence: mostly settled

The Astean Empire is the continent's most prominent surviving imperial power. Its register draws loosely from Roman, Holy Roman, and Prussian influences, but it has had eight centuries to become its own thing.

Common Astean traits:

- institutional weight
- legalism
- old roads and old claims
- ceremonial and military prestige
- layered bureaucracy
- confidence in infrastructure, scholarship, and statecraft

Astean content should remember that the empire is old, not new. It is not only conquest in motion. It is also archives, titles, schools, ruined monuments, officials, reforms, nostalgias, and arguments about what the empire still is.

Variation matters. Not every Astean is devoutly imperial, and not every imperial city feels the same.

## 4. Dwarves

Current confidence: mostly settled

Dwarves are a recognizable people, not a monoculture.

Common dwarven tendencies:

- comfort with routine and repetition
- strong respect for reliability
- literal-minded speech, with a tendency to miss figurative language
- love of retelling favorite stories
- very deep individual expertise
- strong rail and underground infrastructure traditions

Rail culture is important, but not every dwarf is personally obsessed with trains. What should remain recognizable is a broader respect for systems that are dependable, repeatable, and well understood.

Dwarves often come across as earnest and precise. They may seem repetitive to outsiders, but from the dwarven point of view repetition is often a form of care, memory, or craftsmanship.

The dwarven rail network complements the Rune Gate network. Dwarven movement infrastructure should feel deliberate, practical, and culturally important.

## 5. Birdfolk

Current confidence: emerging canon

Birdfolk are highly social. They often prefer dense flock life and can be exceptionally outgoing, including toward people of other races.

Common birdfolk tendencies:

- easy sociability
- curiosity about strangers
- flock-minded comfort in numbers
- preference for lively conversation and social stimulation
- attraction to high roosting places

Birdfolk settlements often cluster in elevated roosts, especially in towns or cities with many other birdfolk. They also travel down freely into mixed settlements and may happily live in human or mixed cities if those cities provide enough social energy.

Birdfolk should feel approachable, talkative, and communal rather than aloof. Their presence can add warmth, movement, gossip, and social momentum to a place.

## 6. Crocodilians

Current confidence: emerging canon

Crocodilians are large crocodile people associated with long dormancy and periodic emergence.

Common crocodilian tendencies:

- years-long hibernation or near-hibernation
- coastal or sandy resting places
- sudden, specific hungers on waking
- narrow but intense purpose during active periods

When a crocodilian emerges, it often wants something highly specific: a certain corpse, a certain material, a certain object, a certain remembered taste. This makes them well suited to one-time side quests, strange bargains, and myth-colored local stories.

Crocodilians should not be written as generic brutes. Their patience, ancient rhythms, and specific appetites matter more than simple aggression.

## 7. Elves

Current confidence: partially settled, mechanically open

Elves are not humanoid. Physically they resemble elk with fractal horns.

They are strongly associated with secrecy, ritual, knowledge-hoarding, and the trade of whispers. Their cults fill the setting role that "religion" often fills in other roguelikes, but their structure and powers need dedicated treatment.

Use `elflore.md` for the deeper elf-specific reference.
