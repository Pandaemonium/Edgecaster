# Edgecaster Aesthetics Guide

## 1. Scope

This document is about how Edgecaster should feel, sound, and speak.

It is not the main source of world history. For world truths, public knowledge, and hidden lore, use `lore_bible.md`. For culture-specific traits and nation-level differences, use `factions_and_nations.md`.

## 2. Tone

Edgecaster is not uniformly grim and not uniformly light. The world should have room for:

- demonic horror
- political intrigue
- natural wonder
- scholarly fascination
- warmth, humor, and occasional absurdity

The key is proportionality. Content should feel native to its region, faction, and situation. Do not smear the same emotional register across the whole continent.

Demonic influence is the main source of the game's darkest imagery. It should feel invasive, corruptive, and often personal. Outside demonic influence, the world should still feel vivid, textured, and worth caring about.

## 3. Diegetic Whimsy

Whimsical, cute, and funny content is welcome, but it should be diegetic rather than meta.

Good fits:

- a birdfolk crowd becoming delightfully overwhelming because everyone wants to talk at once
- a dwarf earnestly missing an idiom and taking it literally
- a demon whose presentation is cute or charming even though its motives are predatory
- a very specific local custom that is funny because it is real to the people practicing it

Avoid:

- wink-at-the-player jokes
- genre parody that treats the world as fake
- modern snark that breaks the setting's texture
- random silliness with no cultural or diegetic basis

## 4. Visual Language

### Healthy Geometry

Fractal geometry is the core mechanic and the core metaphor. Healthy geometry should feel:

- structured
- legible
- alive
- rewarding to study

A strong pattern is not just complicated. It has readable order and satisfying internal logic.

### Corrupted Geometry

Demonic corruption makes geometry wrong. Corrupted geometry should feel:

- bent
- self-intersecting
- unstable
- asymmetrically wrong
- difficult to trust even when it is still readable

The contrast between healthy and corrupted geometry should be visible. If corruption does not change the look and feel of form, it loses too much of its power.

### Material World

The world should feel materially grounded even when its logic is magical. Use stone, metal, wood, crystal, smoke, blood, cloth, bone, ash, frost, and living growth as real substances with weight and implication.

Do not let everything drift into generic fantasy glow.

## 5. Regional and Cultural Variation

Different peoples and factions should have distinct aesthetic registers. The empire, dwarves, birdfolk, crocodilians, elves, scholars, merchants, cultists, and corrupted ruins should not all sound or look like minor variations of one house style.

When in doubt:

- start with the local culture
- then the local material conditions
- then the local magical or political situation
- then the game's broader tone

## 6. Writing Voice and Dialogue

Write in a grounded, specific, in-world register.

- Prefer clear, vivid phrasing over faux-archaic fog.
- Let characters sound like people with histories, trades, loyalties, and habits.
- Use elevated language when a speaker, ritual, or text has earned it.
- Keep exposition under control. The world should feel deep without every line trying to sound mythic.

Dialogue should reflect culture. For example:

- dwarves often trend literal and may miss figurative language
- birdfolk should usually feel socially open and easy to approach
- Astean speech can carry institutional weight, legalism, or imperial polish
- demonic speech can range from seductive to absurd to terrifying, but should still feel intentional

## 7. Naming Conventions

### Hard Rules

- Do not use historical mathematician names as in-world parlance. `Koch`, `Lorenz`, `Barnsley`, `Mandelbrot`, and similar names are implementation-level references, not player-facing terminology.
- Currency is `bismuth`, not gold.

### Naming Feel

Good names usually draw from:

- physical or geometric qualities
- cultural or factional context
- real materials or phenomena in the world

Examples of the desired texture:

- `Choking Vines`
- `Palm Burst`
- `Wind Rush`
- `Rune Anchor`
- `Coherence Crystal`

Avoid names that feel:

- generically medieval-fantasy
- purely technical
- disconnected from the culture that coined them

Ask: does this name belong to this world?

## 8. Audio

Sound follows geometry. The rune audio system should reinforce the claim that geometry is real and consequential.

- Large patterns should feel heavier than small ones.
- Healthy and corrupted patterns should not sound the same.
- Sound should help the player feel shape, density, and strain.

Audio should deepen the setting, not sit on top of it like decorative wallpaper.

## 9. Spoiler Discipline

The game's macro scale and body-swap ambitions should arrive as discovery, not front-loaded marketing inside the fiction.

Safe early-game emphasis:

- grounded travel
- Rune Gates
- demons
- local politics
- body-based rune magic

Use caution with:

- explicit hints that cities, armies, or macro-entities can become inhabitable bodies
- tutorial or codex text that explains the cosmic scope too early

The player should be allowed to feel that the world keeps opening underneath them.

## 10. Style Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- making every region equally demonic
- flattening all cultures into one voice
- explaining the world's weirdest truths too early
- treating whimsy as permission for meta humor
- using generic fantasy naming when a more specific in-world name is possible
- making every magical effect a glowing abstraction with no material identity

## 11. Guiding Questions for New Content

When adding an ability, enemy, item, location, quest, or conversation, ask:

- Does the tone fit the faction or region this belongs to, not just the game in general?
- If geometry is involved, is it interesting to look at? Does corruption visibly break it?
- Does the material palette feel grounded?
- Is any humor diegetic rather than immersion-breaking?
- Does any in-world name accidentally use implementation jargon or historical mathematician names?
- If this hints at cosmic scale, is the player ready for that revelation?
- If this involves demons, does the individual demon have character, or is it just generic evil?
- Does the writing sound like it belongs to this world?
